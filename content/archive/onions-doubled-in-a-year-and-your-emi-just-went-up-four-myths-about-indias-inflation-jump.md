*In January, India's inflation was below 3%. By September it was 5.54%, and the numbers behind that jump say more about your kitchen and your loan than the headline does.*

In June we wrote about [why everything felt expensive when official inflation was near 3%](/explainers/indias-inflation-is-at-3-percent-so-why-does-everything-feel-more-expensive-than-ever). Five months later, the official number has caught up with the feeling. Retail inflation, which measures how much more a typical basket of household goods costs than a year ago, rose to **5.54%** in September, according to the [Ministry of Statistics (MoSPI)](https://www.mospi.gov.in/uploads/latestreleasesfiles/1791801549227-Press%20Release%20for%20the%20month%20of%20September,%202026.pdf). Food inflation was **7.74%**.

Then, on October 7, the Reserve Bank of India raised its repo rate for the first time since 2023. The repo rate is the interest rate at which the RBI lends to banks, and most loan rates in the country take their cue from it.

A lot of half-truths travel with news like this. Here are four of them, checked against the official data.

## Myth 1: "Inflation at 5.54% means everything costs about 5.54% more"

It does not. The 5.54% is an average across hundreds of items, weighted by how much a typical household spends on each. Inside that average, prices are moving in wildly different directions.

MoSPI's own release lists the items that rose fastest and slowest in September. Onion prices were up **108.09%** from a year earlier, which means they had more than doubled. Garlic was up **54.62%** and ginger **62.85%**. Silver jewellery rose **87.80%**. Meanwhile potatoes were **11.50%** cheaper than a year ago and tomatoes **8.62%** cheaper.

```stats
108% | rise in onion prices, September 2026 vs a year earlier
7.74% | food inflation in September
-11.5% | change in potato prices over the same year
9.36% | inflation in restaurants and hotels
```

Think of it like a class average in an exam. A class average of 55 can hide one student scoring 100 and another scoring 10. Your personal inflation depends on what you buy. A family that eats out often faced **9.36%** inflation on restaurant meals, while health costs rose just **1.40%**, per the same release.

## Myth 2: "The RBI raised rates, so onion prices will come down"

A rate hike does not grow more onions. The RBI's own [policy resolution](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63742) says the rise in inflation came mainly from food and fuel, and it lists the risks as a deficient south-west monsoon, strong El Niño conditions and sharp swings in crude oil prices linked to the West Asia conflict. None of those are things an interest rate can fix directly.

The crude part is large. According to a footnote in the [Governor's statement](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63744), the Indian basket of crude oil averaged **$116.1 a barrel** in September, up from $90.2 in August. We covered [who absorbs that cost at the petrol pump](/explainers/crude-is-at-113-dollars-your-petrol-price-has-not-moved-someone-is-paying-the-difference) late last month.

So why hike at all? A costlier loan makes people and businesses borrow and spend a little less, which over months cools demand across the economy. It also signals that the RBI will not let high prices settle in. Governor Sanjay Malhotra put it bluntly: inflation and its outlook "are not benign as they were last year," and "rate cuts are off the table in the near term." ICRA's chief economist Aditi Nayar told [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/economy/story/monsoon-woes-crude-concerns-inflation-worries-may-cloud-indias-second-half-growth-momentum-564764-2026-10-30) that "crude certainly is not in RBI or the government's control," and warned that hikes not warranted by domestic conditions would choke growth. That is the tension the RBI is managing: a tool that works on demand, aimed at a problem that is mostly about supply.

## Myth 3: "My EMI went up the day the RBI announced the hike"

Only for some loans, and not on the same day. Loans priced off an external benchmark, usually the repo rate itself, move when the repo moves. Banks announce a new benchmark rate and pass it on from the next reset.

This time several banks moved within a day. Punjab National Bank and Indian Overseas Bank raised their external benchmark lending rates from **8.10% to 8.35%**, Indian Bank from 7.95% to 8.20%, and Karur Vysya Bank from 8.55% to 8.80%, all effective October 8, as reported by the [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/your-home-loan-emi-may-rise-from-october-8-these-6-banks-have-increased-lending-rates-after-rbis-big-move).

What does 0.25 points mean in rupees? On a ₹30 lakh loan with 20 years left, moving from 8.10% to 8.35% raises the monthly EMI from about ₹25,280 to about ₹25,750, an increase of roughly **₹470** a month (our calculation, using the standard EMI formula). Whether your EMI or your remaining tenure changes depends on your loan terms, so the loan statement is the place to check.

## Myth 4: "Higher rates mean my FD will pay more right away"

The data says not yet. Over July and August, before the hike, the average interest rate banks paid on fresh term deposits actually fell by **28 basis points** (0.28 points), while the average rate on fresh loans rose by 8 basis points, according to the RBI Governor's statement. The RBI linked the drop in deposit rates to a flood of foreign currency deposits that left banks flush with cash.

Banks raise deposit rates when they need money. Loans reset almost automatically; deposits wait for competition. So the lag tends to favour the bank, at least at first.

## The number everyone is waiting for

MoSPI publishes October's inflation figure on **November 12**. The month-by-month climb this year has been steady:

```bars
India's retail inflation, 2026
Year-on-year change in the Consumer Price Index, %
January | 2.74 | 2.74%
March | 3.40 | 3.40%
May | 3.93 | 3.93%
July | 4.45 | 4.45%
August | 4.83 | 4.83%
September | 5.54 | 5.54%
Source: MoSPI CPI release (base 2024=100), October 12, 2026
```

ICRA expects October to come in at **6.1%**, with food and beverages inflation above 8%, according to a note reported by [BusinessLine](https://www.tradingview.com/news/moodys:5764e5190fff9:0-retail-inflation-may-breach-6-in-october-amid-rising-food-prices/). ICRA says part of that jump is a statistical quirk, an unfavourable base from last year's GST rate rationalisation. MoSPI's own table shows the price index did not move at all between September and October 2025, so this October is being compared with a flat month. Barclays economists, quoted in the same report, think the pass-through from wholesale to retail prices "may be moderate." The RBI has a target of 4% and a tolerance band of 2% to 6%. BusinessLine notes that a reading above 6% would be the first breach of that band since October 2024.

The RBI's next policy meeting is on December 2 to 4. HSBC and Goldman Sachs expect another hike, according to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/07/india-rbi-interest-rates-inflation.html), and Nayar expects another 0.25 points. None of that is settled until the committee votes.

If prices keep running ahead of rates, the RBI also has the rupee to watch. We looked at [what it costs the RBI to defend the currency](/explainers/the-rbi-is-spending-billions-a-week-just-to-keep-the-rupee-from-falling) in August.
