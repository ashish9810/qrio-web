*Two companies agreed on Sunday to swap money and businesses, and on Monday the market punished both of them, for opposite reasons.*

On Monday, November 23, the Sensex fell **1,483.72 points**, or 1.77%, to close at 82,435.99, as [IndiaBlooms reported](https://www.indiablooms.com/finance/stock-market-bloodbath-sensex-crashes-1484-points-investors-lose-rs639-lakh-crore/details). The single biggest loser in the Nifty 50 was Tata Steel, down **over 8%**. Outside the index, APL Apollo Tubes, India's best known maker of steel pipes and hollow sections, fell more than **21%**, which [CNBC TV18 called](https://www.tradingview.com/news/cnbctv:43d687d89094b:0/) its steepest single day fall in 20 years.

Both drops came from one deal. To see why it scared investors in both companies, it helps to follow the money as it moves, one transfer at a time.

## Transfer one: APL Apollo pays Tata Steel ₹28,000 crore

Tata Steel is selling two businesses to APL Apollo. The first is its steel tubes business, the pipes used in buildings, fences, solar panel frames and furniture. The second is Tata Steel Colors, which makes colour coated steel sheets, the bright roofing you see on warehouses and factory sheds.

The combined price is **₹28,000 crore**, according to [Tata Steel's press release](https://www.tatasteel.com/newsroom/press-releases/india/2026/tata-steel-and-promoters-of-apl-apollo-enter-into-a-comprehensive-agreement-to-build-an-indian-steel-tubes-and-colour-coated-products-powerhouse-targeting-155-million-tonnes-of-capacity/). [IIFL](https://www.indiainfoline.com/news/companies/why-tata-steel-and-apl-apollo-tyres-are-down-today) and [INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/stocks/tata-steel-apl-apollo-deal-and-why-share-crashed) both split it as about **₹11,700 crore** for tubes and **₹16,300 crore** for Colors.

Here is the catch. APL Apollo does not have that money lying around. Brokerage Emkay Global noted it had about **₹1,150 crore** in cash and a net worth of about **₹5,740 crore** at the end of September, as quoted by [Univest](https://univest.in/blogs/apl-apollo-tubes-tata-steel-block-deal-acquisition). Tata Steel's release says APL Apollo will first use "acquisition financing", a polite word for a big loan, and later replace it with long term capital, which could mean selling new shares.

Think of a family that owns a ₹50 lakh flat buying the ₹30 lakh flat next door, with almost all of it on a loan. After Monday's fall, APL Apollo's whole stock market value was under **₹46,100 crore**, per [Moneycontrol](https://www.tradingview.com/news/moneycontrol:b5e2e7803094b:0-apl-apollo-tubes-shares-plunge-15-tata-steel-falls-6-as-rs-5-825-crore-block-deal-follows-acquisition-news/). By our arithmetic, the ₹28,000 crore bill is about **60%** of that.

## Transfer two: Tata Steel pays the APL Apollo founders ₹15,510 crore

Now the money flows back. Tata Steel is buying the APL Apollo promoters' stake, held through S Gupta Holding, at **₹2,100 a share**, for about **₹15,510 crore**. The press release calls it a 26.6% stake. The exchange filings, measured against the company's slightly larger future share count, put it at **26.38%**, and split it into three slices, as Moneycontrol and IIFL both reported:

```timeline
November 23, 2026 | 9.91% bought through a block deal worth about ₹5,825 crore, held in escrow with no voting rights for now
After approvals | 11.90% moved off market
By March 31, 2028 | The last 4.57%, subject to conditions
```

The first slice changed hands on Monday itself. The founding promoter group has said it intends to be reclassified as ordinary public shareholders, according to Moneycontrol.

## Transfer three: Tata Steel offers you ₹2,301.40 a share

A stake this large triggers a mandatory open offer under SEBI's takeover rules. That is a standing invitation to every other shareholder to sell to the buyer at a fixed price. Tata Steel's offer is for another **26%** of APL Apollo at **₹2,301.40 a share**, worth up to **₹16,756 crore**. If it is fully taken up, Tata Steel would own **52.38%** and control the company. Kotak Mahindra Capital is managing the offer.

That price looks generous. APL Apollo closed Monday at about **₹1,659**, per Moneycontrol, so the offer is roughly **39% higher**, by our arithmetic. Why would anyone sell on the market at ₹1,659 when Tata will pay ₹2,301?

Two reasons. First, the offer is limited. If promoters held about 26% of APL Apollo, the public holds roughly three quarters. If every one of them tendered, only about **one share in three** would be accepted, by our rough arithmetic, and the rest would go back to the owner at whatever the market price is then. Second, it is slow. The offer can only be completed after regulatory approvals, and we could not find a confirmed date for it.

## Where the money ends up

Put the three transfers side by side and something striking appears.

```flow
How the money moves
APL Apollo borrows and pays Tata Steel ₹28,000 crore for two businesses
Tata Steel pays the APL Apollo founders ₹15,510 crore for their stake
Tata Steel pays public shareholders up to ₹16,756 crore in the open offer
Net for Tata Steel: up to about ₹4,266 crore spent, for control of APL Apollo and its own old businesses inside it
```

That last line is our own arithmetic: ₹15,510 crore plus ₹16,756 crore is ₹32,266 crore, minus the ₹28,000 crore Tata Steel receives. If few shareholders accept the open offer, Tata Steel could even end up with more cash than it started with. In plain words, a large part of the price of APL Apollo is being paid with APL Apollo's own borrowed money.

## Why APL Apollo shareholders ran

From their side, the company is taking on a huge loan to buy businesses whose price works out to about **24.8 times** their yearly operating profit before interest and depreciation (EBITDA), per INDmoney. That is expensive for steel. IIFL reports Tata Steel Colors made a profit of about **₹85 crore** in FY26 on revenue of about **₹4,680 crore**, and is being sold for ₹16,300 crore. Higher interest costs, or a fresh share sale to repay the loan, could both hurt existing owners. Not everyone is gloomy. Emkay called the deal transformational and said it could expand APL Apollo's revenue base by about **70%**. JM Financial kept an "Add" rating with a target of **₹2,300**, while flagging funding needs, per Univest.

## Why Tata Steel shareholders ran too

Tata Steel gets a big cheque, but gives up businesses that brought in about **₹16,100 crore** of revenue in FY26, roughly **11%** of its India revenue, according to Emkay. It also commits up to ₹32,266 crore of its own cash before the APL Apollo money arrives. JM Financial saw a bright side: the ₹28,000 crore equals about **31%** of Tata Steel's consolidated net debt of **₹91,423 crore**. CNBC TV18 also cited weak metal prices as pressure on the stock that day.

Tata Steel's CFO Koushik Chatterjee said in the release that this is "not just an acquisition of a very profitable and efficient company", and the group is targeting **15.5 million tonnes** of tubes and colour coated capacity by 2035, up from 7.5 million tonnes once the deal closes. The company expects to finish within FY2027, though one slice of the promoter stake can move as late as March 2028.

It is a little like the [Affle and Perion deal](/explainers/affle-is-paying-420-million-for-perion-more-than-half-of-it-is-already-in-perions-bank), where half the headline price was already sitting in the target's bank. Here, the money goes round in a circle, and Monday's fall was the market saying it is not yet sure who comes out ahead.
