*If you flew IndiGo this monsoon and felt the fare was steep, you were right. You paid about a third more per kilometre than a year ago. The airline still lost money. Follow the rupees and you find out why.*

On Thursday, InterGlobe Aviation, the company that runs IndiGo, reported its results for the July to September quarter. The headline looks like a contradiction:

```stats
+25.9% | revenue, to ₹23,364 crore
₹1,879 Cr | net loss for the quarter
+67.2% | jump in the jet fuel bill
```

How does a company bring in a quarter more money and still end up deep in the red? Let us walk through the money the way you would check a household budget: what came in, what went out, and which bill broke it.

## What came in: you paid more, but fewer of you flew

IndiGo carried **27.5 million passengers** in the quarter, 4.7% fewer than a year earlier, according to [Newsdrum](https://www.newsdrum.in/business/indigo-q2-loss-narrows-to-rs-1879-crore-as-fuel-costs-jump-67-12765528). It also flew slightly less: its capacity, measured in seats multiplied by kilometres flown, fell 1% to 40.8 billion. Planes were a little emptier too, with 80.3% of seats filled compared with 82.5% a year ago.

So how did revenue go up 26%? Price. Airlines measure this as "yield", the average amount a passenger pays to fly one kilometre. IndiGo's yield rose **34.3%**, from ₹4.69 to ₹6.30.

On a 1,000 km trip, that is the difference between paying roughly ₹4,690 and ₹6,300 in fare (a simplification, but it shows the scale). Ticket revenue climbed 29.3% to ₹20,652 crore. Extras like seats, meals and baggage added another ₹2,481 crore.

In short: fewer people flew, and the ones who did paid a lot more.

## What went out: one bill ate the increase

Total expenses rose 18.6% to **₹26,188 crore**. That is less than revenue grew, which sounds like good news. The problem is where the extra spending went.

```bars
IndiGo's jet fuel bill
July to September, ₹ crore
2025 | 5962 | ₹5,962 Cr
2026 | 9967 | ₹9,967 Cr
Source: InterGlobe Aviation results via Newsdrum
```

The jet fuel bill alone went up by about **₹4,005 crore** in a year. Revenue went up by about ₹4,809 crore. So fuel swallowed more than four out of every five extra rupees that the higher fares brought in. Fuel cost per seat kilometre rose 68.9% to ₹2.44, per [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/interglobe-aviation-q2-net-loss-at-rs-1879-crore-revenue-rises-564571-2026-10-29).

Why did fuel cost so much more? Oil. The conflict in West Asia has disrupted shipping through the [Strait of Hormuz](/explainers/the-strait-of-hormuz-reopened-this-week-the-ships-still-wont-go), and crude prices have spent much of 2026 far above last year's levels. CEO Willie Walsh said the quarter saw *"exceptionally elevated fuel costs and continued geopolitical turmoil"*, and that cutting capacity and managing fares only partly made up for it.

There is a policy twist too. From April to early June, the government capped aviation fuel prices at 25% above their March level. From 9 June it moved to a Price Stabilisation Fund scheme, and IndiGo chose not to join it, according to Newsdrum.

## The bill that was not about flying at all

There is one more line, and it has nothing to do with planes. IndiGo leases many of its aircraft and pays for them in dollars. When the rupee weakens, those dollar bills cost more rupees, and the accounts show a "foreign exchange loss".

This quarter that loss was **₹1,333 crore**. Strip it out, and IndiGo's loss from actually running the airline was about **₹724 crore**. A year ago, on the same basis, it made a profit of ₹104 crore.

Here is the strange part. The forex loss a year ago was much bigger, ₹2,892 crore. That is why the headline loss looks smaller this year (it was ₹2,582 crore last year) even though the airline business itself went from a small profit to a real loss. A "narrower loss" headline can hide a worse quarter.

## So is IndiGo in trouble?

Not in the way that word usually means. IndiGo had about **₹52,685 crore** in cash at the end of September, including about ₹39,929 crore it is free to use. Its total debt, including the lease payments it owes on planes, was about ₹87,303 crore. It added 10 aircraft in the quarter and now flies 442, with a peak of 2,020 flights a day.

But it is now two loss-making quarters in a row. The April to June quarter lost ₹237.6 crore, so this one is about eight times worse. July to September is usually the weakest season for Indian airlines because of the monsoon, which helps explain part of it. The company expects capacity to grow only in the low single digits from October to December, and it announced no dividend.

## What this means for your next ticket

When an airline's single biggest cost jumps two thirds and it is already losing money, it has two levers: fly less, and charge more. IndiGo has already pulled both. As long as oil stays high, cheap fares are unlikely to be the norm.
