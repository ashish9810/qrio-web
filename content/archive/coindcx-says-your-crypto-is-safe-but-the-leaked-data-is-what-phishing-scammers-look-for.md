*No coins left CoinDCX this time, but a list of names, emails and bank names did, and that is the raw material of a convincing scam.*

If you use CoinDCX, one of India's biggest crypto exchanges, you may have seen the email this week. The short version: an outside company that sent CoinDCX's app notifications was broken into, and some customer details were taken. Your coins, the company says, are where you left them.

That sounds like the end of it. It is not quite. Let us go through what is confirmed, what is still unknown, and what the leak actually makes possible.

## What CoinDCX has confirmed

The breach happened at **Plotline**, a company CoinDCX used to show in-app messages and pop-ups, as [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/breach-at-external-vendor-exposes-coindcx-user-data/) and [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/coindcx-plotline-customer-data-breach/) report. Firms like this are called vendors: outside companies that a business hires for one job, the way a restaurant might hire a delivery service.

According to CoinDCX's notice, three pieces of customer data were exposed:

- your **name**
- your **email address**
- the **name of the bank** linked to your CoinDCX account

The bank account number was not on that list, both crypto.news and [CoinGabbar](https://www.coingabbar.com/en/coindcx-plotline-data-breach) note. Nor were passwords, private keys or wallet balances.

Someone without permission got into Plotline's systems twice: **October 24 to 26** and **October 30 to 31, 2026**. Plotline told CoinDCX on **October 31**. CoinDCX posted its public notice on X on **November 17**, per crypto.news. Inc42 says affected users were also emailed, but the date of those emails is not public.

CoinDCX says Plotline "has no access to CoinDCX internal databases, trade execution engines, or user wallets," and that customer funds are "entirely secure and untouched." A spokesperson told Inc42 the vendor "experienced a security incident that exposed limited user contact details." The exchange says it has confirmed with Plotline that the incident is contained and has started reviewing Plotline's security and its own vendor checks.

## What nobody has told us yet

**How many people.** CoinDCX says a "small number" of customers. It has not given a figure. For scale, TechCrunch reported in July 2025 that the exchange had more than **16 million** users.

**What the attacker did with the data.** Inc42 notes CoinDCX has not said whether the data was copied, downloaded or passed on, or who was behind the access.

**Whether the odd login alerts are linked.** Some users posted screenshots of "sudden login alert" messages from CoinDCX, one referencing a new login on November 16 at 10:48 pm. crypto.news says it could not establish any link between those alerts and the Plotline breach. That question is still open.

## Why a name and a bank name matter

On its own, your name and email are not secret. Plenty of companies have them. What changes here is the combination.

A random scam email says "Dear customer, your account is blocked." This one can say "Hi Priya, your CoinDCX withdrawal to your bank is on hold, verify your KYC here." It knows who you are, where you trade and where your money lands. Think of it like a stranger at your door who already knows your name and your landlord's name: you are far more likely to open up.

That is why the useful question is not "was my money stolen?" but "will I be targeted next?" If you were on the list, the honest answer is: possibly, and the scam will look real.

## This is not CoinDCX's first warning

By our count, this is the fourth security incident at the exchange in about 16 months. Two cost the company real money. Two exposed customer details through vendors.

```timeline
CoinDCX's security incidents since mid-2025
Jul 19, 2025 | Attackers drain about $44 million from an internal account used for liquidity on a partner exchange; CoinDCX covers it from its treasury
Nov 25, 2025 | Analytics vendor Mixpanel tells CoinDCX some user data was accessed
Oct 21, 2026 | About 16 million USDT moves out of old wallets retired after the 2025 attack
Oct 24 to 31, 2026 | Unauthorised access at messaging vendor Plotline, in two windows
Nov 17, 2026 | CoinDCX posts its notice about the Plotline breach
```

The July 2025 attack hit an account "used only for liquidity provisioning on a partner exchange," CEO Sumit Gupta said, as reported by [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/21/indian-crypto-exchange-coindcx-confirms-44-million-stolen-during-hack). The company said no customer funds were affected and it would absorb the loss.

In November 2025, CoinDCX said a breach at [Mixpanel](https://www.freepressjournal.in/tech/coindcx-confirms-user-data-exposure-after-mixpanel-breach-funds-safe), a vendor that tracks how people use apps, exposed some user data. That incident hit Mixpanel's customers broadly, not CoinDCX alone.

The October 2026 loss is the messiest. After the 2025 attack, CoinDCX stopped using some wallet addresses, but some users kept sending deposits to them. A background process meant to sweep those deposits into safe wallets stopped working, so money piled up, and about **15.99 million USDT** (USDT is a crypto token meant to track the US dollar) was taken, per [Blockonomi](https://blockonomi.com/coindcx-hit-by-15-99m-crypto-hack-tether-steps-in-to-freeze-funds/). On the morning the reports spread, Gupta said CoinDCX wallets "had not been breached," [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/old-wallet-addresses-trap-coindcx-users-triggering-massive-losses/) reported, before the company later confirmed the loss and said it "could have been more proactive" in telling people. CoinDCX says it covered the loss and customer balances were credited. Tether, the company behind USDT, froze about **$2.2 million** of it, per Blockonomi.

In every case, CoinDCX says customer money was protected, and it absorbed the losses itself. The pattern people are questioning is not lost savings. It is how many doors keep opening, and how many of them belong to other companies.

## The vendor problem, in plain words

A modern app is not one company's code. It is a stack of outside services: one sends notifications, one tracks clicks, one handles support chat. Each one needs some of your data to do its job, and each one is another door a thief can try. A [Plotline case study](https://crypto.news/coindcx-plotline-customer-data-breach/) cited by crypto.news describes more than 20 campaigns it ran inside the CoinDCX app over three months.

India's data protection rules, notified in November 2025, require a company to tell each affected person about a breach "without delay" and in plain language, [PTI reported via The Week](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/11/14/del148-biz-dpdp-rules-ld-govt.html). The rules become fully operational about 18 months after notification. ## If you have a CoinDCX account, do this

No need to panic. A few habits go a long way:

1. **Do not click links in emails or SMS about CoinDCX, your bank or KYC.** Open the app or type the website yourself.
2. **Never share an OTP, password or seed phrase** (the secret words that unlock a crypto wallet). No genuine exchange or bank needs them from you.
3. **Turn on two-factor authentication** and check your recent logins. If one looks unfamiliar, change your password.
4. **If you lose money to a scam, call 1930**, India's cyber fraud helpline, or file at cybercrime.gov.in. The helpline is toll-free, as [The Tribune reported](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/punjab/dial-1930-to-report-financial-frauds-punjab-police-384126/amp).
