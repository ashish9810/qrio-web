*Jio's first day on the stock market was a party. But the most interesting part of this IPO is not the 21% jump. It is who sold nothing, and where the money is going.*

On Wednesday morning, 28 October 2026, shares of Jio Platforms started trading on the BSE and NSE at **₹1,358**. People who had been allotted shares at the IPO price of **₹1,119** were sitting on a gain of 21.36% before most offices had opened. By the close, the stock was at **₹1,417.35** on the BSE, up 26.66% on the day, according to [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/jio-platforms-lists-at-21-36-premium-after-indias-largest-ipo).

If you have heard about this listing from five different WhatsApp groups and still are not sure what actually happened, here are the five questions that matter.

## 1. How big was this, really?

Bigger than anything India has done before. Jio raised **₹30,213 crore**, about $3.2 billion. The previous record holder was Hyundai Motor India, which raised ₹27,870 crore in October 2024, as listed in a comparison by [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/market-news/ipo/jio-platforms-ipo-breaks-records-how-hyundai-nse-lic-and-other-mega-issues-fared-on-day-1/article-202176/).

```bars

Hyundai Motor India (2024) | 27870 | ₹27,870 Cr
```

The demand was even bigger than the size. Bids came in for **22.19 times** the shares on offer, worth more than $77 billion, from more than **13.8 million applicants**, per [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/jio-shares-surge-21-in-market-debut-after-blockbuster-india-ipo/). That is roughly 1.38 crore people trying to buy a piece of one company in the same three days. Most of them got nothing, because there simply were not enough shares to go around.

## 2. Did Meta and Google cash out?

No, and this is the detail most people miss.

Back in 2020, Meta (then Facebook) put $5.7 billion into Jio and Google put in $4.5 billion, alongside investors like General Atlantic, Silver Lake and Vista. A big IPO is usually the moment early investors sell some of their stake to the public. That sale is called an "offer for sale", and the money goes to the sellers, not the company.

Jio's IPO had **no offer for sale at all**. It was entirely new shares. Nobody who already owned Jio sold a single share, including Meta and Google. Every rupee raised went to Jio itself.

That is unusual for an issue this size, and it tells you something. The existing owners, including Reliance, chose to keep their full stakes rather than take money off the table at a valuation of more than ₹10 lakh crore.

## 3. So what is Jio doing with ₹30,213 crore?

Mostly paying off loans.

Of the money raised, **₹27,500 crore** goes towards prepaying borrowings of Reliance Jio Infocomm, the telecom company inside Jio Platforms. The rest is for general corporate purposes. As of 30 June, Jio's total borrowings stood at about ₹61,370 crore, according to TechCrunch.

```stats
₹27,500 Cr | of the IPO money goes to prepaying telecom debt
₹30,049 Cr | Jio's net profit in FY26, up 15.1%
53.3 Cr | customers on Reliance Jio Infocomm (June 2026)
```

Think of it like a family that is already earning well, sells a slice of the house to friends, and uses the money to clear most of the home loan. The business does not change overnight. But with far less interest to pay, more of what it earns stays as profit. That is a large part of what investors were betting on.

The business itself is big and still growing. In FY26, Jio's operating revenue rose almost 15% to **₹1.47 lakh crore** and net profit rose 15.1% to **₹30,049 crore**, per Inc42. Average revenue per user went up to ₹215.60 a month from ₹208.80 a year earlier, and the average customer now uses **43.7 GB of data a month**, up from 37 GB.

## 4. Why did the price jump so much on day one?

Simple supply and demand. When 22 times more money chases the shares than there are shares available, the people who did not get any try to buy on the first day. That pushes the price up.

There is a twist, though. Brokerage Motilal Oswal started covering the stock with a "buy" rating and a target price of **₹1,350**, per Inc42. The shares opened above that and closed around ₹1,417. In other words, on day one the market had already gone past where at least one well-known analyst thought the stock was worth going over the coming months. That does not mean the price will fall. It means a lot of optimism was priced in very quickly.

At the closing price, Jio was worth about **₹13.05 lakh crore**, which made it India's second most valuable listed company, behind its own parent Reliance Industries. In 2020, when Meta and Google invested, Jio was valued at up to ₹4.91 lakh crore. That is a big jump in six years.

## 5. I did not get shares. Did I miss out?

Maybe, maybe not, and anyone who tells you for sure is guessing.

A first-day gain says how excited people were on one Wednesday. It says very little about where a stock will be in three years. Some of India's most hyped listings have done brilliantly since. Others, like Paytm in 2021, fell far below their IPO price for a long time. Jio's numbers are strong, but its price now assumes they stay strong.

What is fair to say is that this listing changed something bigger than Jio. Akash Ambani put it this way: *"My dream is that one day, when the world counts its great technology companies, one of them will be from India."* For the 1.38 crore people who applied, many of them first-time IPO investors, this was the moment Indian tech stopped being something you read about and became something you could try to own.
