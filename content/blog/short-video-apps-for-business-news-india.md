**The short answer:** there is no single short video app for business news in India yet, so the honest pick is a pair. Use a short text app like Inshorts or Finshots for the daily facts, and follow a few business channels inside YouTube for video. Below is what each option actually costs you in time, money and attention.

We checked every app's own Google Play listing on 30 October 2026 while writing this. Ratings and download counts change, so treat them as a snapshot rather than a score.

## Why is this list so short?

Because "short video app in India" usually means entertainment. Search that phrase and you get roundups of Moj, Chingari and Roposo, which are built for music, comedy and dance clips rather than markets or geopolitics. The category that exists for business news is mostly text.

That gap matters more than it used to. In the Reuters Institute's [Digital News Report 2026](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/india), about 58 per cent of Indian respondents said they rely on YouTube for news, with WhatsApp second at 56 per cent. The same report found 52 per cent say they avoid the news sometimes or often, and trust in news sat at 39 per cent, four points down on the year before. [Newslaundry's write-up](https://www.newslaundry.com/2026/06/18/58-news-from-youtube-56-via-whatsapp-reuters-report-on-indias-news-consumption-habits) reports the same figures.

One caveat the report states plainly: the India sample is an online survey of mainly English-speaking news users, so it is not nationally representative and under-represents TV and print. Read it as a picture of urban online India, which is probably you if you are reading this.

So people already want news as video. The apps built for it are just mostly about something else.

## Inshorts: still the fastest way to catch up

[Inshorts](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nis.app) summarises stories from multiple sources into 60 words or fewer, in English or Hindi, with sections for business, technology, startups and world news. Its listing shows 4.4 stars from roughly 6.16 lakh reviews and more than 1 crore downloads, and it was updated on 27 October 2026.

**Good for:** knowing what happened before a meeting. Five minutes gets you the day.

**Less good for:** understanding why it happened. Sixty words gives you the fact and nothing underneath it. The app also carries ads.

## Finshots: one story a day, properly told

[Finshots](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.finception.finshots.android), made by Finception, sends one financial story a day in plain English, tagged by areas like policy, economy and startups. Its listing claims a read of under three minutes, says the reading experience is ad free, and states there are no in-app purchases. It shows 4.8 stars from about 20.6 thousand reviews and 5 lakh plus downloads.

Worth noting: the listing's own copy still says "100K+ downloads & 4.9 stars", which no longer matches what Play displays. Stale marketing copy, not a red flag.

**Good for:** actually following a story to its end. The daily single story is the whole point.

**Less good for:** breadth. One story a day means you will miss things. Pair it with something faster.

## Seekho: real short video, mostly in Hindi

[Seekho](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.seekho.android) is the closest big Indian app to a vertical video feed about money and business. Its categories include share market, business growth and online earning, and it is largely in Hindi. The listing shows 4.5 stars from about 13.9 lakh reviews and over 100 million downloads, updated on 29 October 2026.

Be clear-eyed about the model, though. The listing shows in-app purchases, and among the most-upvoted reviews on the page is one from September 2025, marked helpful by more than 13,500 people, saying the content is locked behind payment and that a one rupee trial leads to a larger charge. A high overall rating and loud complaints about a paywall can both be true at once.

**Good for:** Hindi-first viewers who want video and do not mind paying.

**Less good for:** anyone expecting the free feed to be the product.

## Moneycontrol: for people who hold stocks

[Moneycontrol](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.divum.MoneyControl), from Network18, is the heavyweight: live market data from BSE, NSE, MCX and NCDEX, portfolio and watchlist tracking, business news and live CNBC streaming. It shows 3.5 stars from about 4.4 lakh reviews and more than 1 crore downloads, with both ads and in-app purchases.

That 3.5 rating is the lowest here, and the app is dense by design. It is a markets terminal that happens to carry news.

**Good for:** anyone tracking a portfolio daily.

**Less good for:** a calm five-minute catch up. It is a lot of screen.

## YouTube: the real answer most people land on

[YouTube](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.youtube) is not a business news app, but given that 58 per cent of Indian respondents told the Reuters Institute they use it for news, it is where most of this already happens. Indian business publications and independent creators both post short vertical videos there.

**Good for:** depth and range, free, and you probably have it installed.

**Less good for:** staying on target. The feed that gets you a clear six-minute breakdown of a trade deal is the same feed that will hand you forty minutes of something else. If that is the loop you are trying to break, our post on [apps to replace your Instagram scroll](/blog/apps-to-replace-instagram-reels) covers the swap in more detail.

## How to put this together in ten minutes a day

You do not need all six. A workable routine:

1. **Mornings, three minutes.** Inshorts for the headline sweep.
2. **Most days, three minutes.** Finshots for the one story worth following.
3. **Two or three times a week, five minutes.** A video on whatever you did not understand that week, from YouTube or a smaller app.
4. **Weekends.** Nothing. The weekly catch up is more useful than daily completeness.

If even that feels like too much, our guide on [how to stay informed in 5 minutes a day](/blog/stay-informed-in-5-minutes-a-day) cuts it down further, and [the best ways to follow Indian business news](/blog/best-ways-to-follow-indian-business-news) goes deeper on sources beyond apps.

The test for any of these is simple. A week later, can you explain one story to someone else? If a story like [how Zomato won by owning the least glamorous thing in tech](/explainers/analysts-said-zomato-would-go-broke-it-won-by-owning-the-least-glamorous-thing-in-tech) stays with you, the app earned its place on your home screen. If the week is a blur, it did not, whatever its rating says.
