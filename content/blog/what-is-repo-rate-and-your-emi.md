**The short answer:** The repo rate is the interest rate at which the Reserve Bank of India lends money to banks. When it goes up, banks raise the rates on floating-rate loans linked to it, so your EMI or your loan tenure goes up too, usually within three months.

That is not a theory right now. On 7 October 2026 the RBI raised the repo rate from 5.25% to 5.50%, its first increase since February 2023. This guide explains what the repo rate is, how a change travels to your home loan and your fixed deposit, and what you can actually do about it. It is general information, not financial advice.

## What is the repo rate, in plain words?

Banks sometimes need to borrow money for a short time, and one place they can borrow from is the RBI. "Repo" is short for repurchase agreement, the kind of short-term loan involved. The interest the RBI charges is the repo rate.

[Kotak Mahindra Bank](https://www.kotak.bank.in/en/stories-in-focus/loans/home-loan/how-the-current-repo-rate-hike-shall-affect-your-home-loan-emis-explained-with-example.html) puts it simply: it is "the rate at which the central bank of India, RBI, lends money to all banks". When it rises, banks pay more to borrow, and they tend to pass that cost on to their own borrowers. Think of it as the wholesale price of money. Your loan rate is the retail price.

The rate is set by the RBI's Monetary Policy Committee, or MPC. As the table further down shows, it has met roughly every two months in recent years, and each meeting ends with a decision to raise, cut or hold.

## Why did the RBI raise the repo rate in October 2026?

Inflation started rising again. The [RBI's policy resolution of 7 October 2026](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63742) said "inflation and its outlook are not benign as they were last year" and that "recalibrating the policy rate is imperative". The MPC voted unanimously for the hike and changed its stance to "calibrated tightening". It also said rate cuts are "off the table in the near term", so the next move can only be a hike or a pause.

The [Governor's statement](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63744) gives the reasons in numbers:

- Retail inflation rose to 4.8% in August, from 4.5% in July.
- Onion prices rose about 85% between the end of June and the end of September.
- The monsoon ended about 13% below normal.
- India's crude oil basket averaged US$116.1 a barrel in September, up from US$82.0 in July.

The RBI now expects inflation to average 5.2% for 2026-27, with the October to December quarter at 6.0%. [SCC Online's summary](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/10/09/rbi-hikes-repo-rate-to-5-50-percent-october-2026/) confirms the new rate, the stance change and the date of the next meeting: 2 to 4 December 2026. We looked at the food side of this in our story on [onion prices and the inflation jump](/explainers/onions-doubled-in-a-year-and-your-emi-just-went-up-four-myths-about-indias-inflation-jump).

## How has the repo rate moved in recent years?

The rate has gone down and up a lot in six years. These are the turning points, from [StableInvestor's repo rate history](https://stableinvestor.com/2021/10/rbi-repo-rate-history-india.html/amp), checked against the RBI's October 2026 release and [IndMoney's report on the December 2025 cut](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/stocks/rbi-monetary-policy-rate-cut-25bps):

| Date | Repo rate | What happened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| May 2020 | 4.00% | Cut during Covid, then held for two years |
| May 2022 to Feb 2023 | 4.00% to 6.50% | Six hikes as inflation rose |
| Feb 2025 to Jun 2025 | 6.50% to 5.50% | Three cuts |
| 5 Dec 2025 | 5.25% | One more cut, total of 1.25 points in 2025 |
| Feb to Aug 2026 | 5.25% | Held at four meetings |
| 7 Oct 2026 | 5.50% | First hike since February 2023 |

Notice the pattern. Borrowers who took a floating-rate home loan in 2021 saw their rate climb by 2.5 points in under a year. That is why it is worth knowing how your own loan is linked.

## How does a repo rate change reach your EMI?

It depends on what your loan is linked to. Look for one of these in your sanction letter.

| Loan type | How it is priced | When a repo change reaches you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repo-linked or external benchmark loan (EBLR, RLLR) | Repo rate plus a fixed spread set by the bank | At the next reset, at least once every three months |
| MCLR-linked loan (older bank loans) | Bank's own internal benchmark plus a spread | Slower, at the reset date in your agreement |
| Fixed-rate loan | One rate for the fixed period | Not at all during the fixed period |

Since 1 October 2019, the RBI has required banks to link all new floating-rate retail loans, such as home and car loans, to an external benchmark. The repo rate is one of the allowed benchmarks. The rule also says the rate "shall be reset at least once in three months", as reported by [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2019/09/04/rbi-asks-banks-to-link-lending-rate-to-external-benchmark.html) and [Business Today](https://businesstoday.in/pti-feed/story/rbi-asks-banks-to-link-lending-rate-to-external-benchmark-226751-2019-09-04) at the time.

Your loan rate is the benchmark plus the bank's spread. If your bank's repo-linked rate is 8.50% and the repo rate rises by 0.25 points, your rate becomes 8.75% at the next reset. The spread stays the same.

This time, some banks moved within a day. The [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/your-home-loan-emi-may-rise-from-october-8-these-6-banks-have-increased-lending-rates-after-rbis-big-move) reported that Punjab National Bank raised its external benchmark lending rate from 8.10% to 8.35%, and Indian Bank its repo-linked rate from 7.95% to 8.20%, both from 8 October 2026.

## How much will your EMI go up?

For most people, a few hundred rupees a month per ₹10 lakh to ₹50 lakh borrowed. Here is our calculation for a 20-year loan where the rate rises from 8.50% to 8.75%:

| Loan amount | EMI at 8.50% | EMI at 8.75% | Extra per month |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ₹30 lakh | ₹26,035 | ₹26,511 | ₹477 |
| ₹50 lakh | ₹43,391 | ₹44,186 | ₹794 |

The [Free Press Journal's own example](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/rbi-repo-rate-hike-how-much-will-emi-rise-on-rs-20-lakh-rs-30-lakh-and-rs-50-lakh-home-loans) uses a lower starting rate of 7.50%, and gets about ₹767 more a month on a ₹50 lakh loan. The exact number depends on your rate and how many years are left.

## Higher EMI or longer tenure: which should you pick?

A lender may keep your EMI the same and stretch the tenure instead. It feels painless, but it costs more.

Take the same ₹50 lakh, 20-year loan. In our calculation:

- **Raise the EMI** by ₹794 and total interest over the loan rises by about ₹1.9 lakh.
- **Keep the EMI and extend the tenure** and you pay for about 12 more months, with total interest up by about ₹5.4 lakh.

That is based on the change applying from the first month, so your numbers will be smaller if you are years into the loan. The direction holds either way: a longer tenure means more months of interest.

You have a say in this. An RBI circular of August 2023 requires lenders to give borrowers, at the time of a reset, the option to raise the EMI, extend the tenure, or a mix of both, and to switch to a fixed rate under the lender's board-approved policy. It also says an extended tenure must not lead to "negative amortisation", where your loan grows instead of shrinking. Details are in [The Tribune's report](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/rbi-to-banks-let-loanees-opt-for-fixed-interest-rate-536077) on the circular and the [Legal 500 summary of the RBI's FAQs](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/finance-and-banking/faqs-on-rbi-circular-resetting-floating-interest-rates-for-emi-based-personal-loans).

## Will your fixed deposit earn more?

Possibly, but not automatically, and not by the same amount. Deposit rates are each bank's own decision.

- **Some lenders moved fast.** Bajaj Finance raised its FD rates by 15 to 40 basis points from 7 October 2026, according to [Goodreturns](https://www.goodreturns.in/personal-finance/rbi-repo-rate-banks-nbfcs-to-hike-fd-interest-rates-by-25-bps-sbi-hdfc-icici-bajaj-finance-fd-rates-1539733.html).
- **Big banks were slower.** [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/investing/hdfc-bank-icici-bank-punjab-national-bank-bank-of-baroda-fixed-deposit-rates-where-they-stand-after-rbi-october-repo-rate-hike/article-201783/) found that ICICI Bank revised its rates from 13 October, while HDFC Bank, PNB and Bank of Baroda were still on rates set before the hike. Upstox also cites SBI Chairman C S Setty as indicating banks may not raise deposit rates for two to three months.

The reason is liquidity. The RBI's statement says banks had a daily surplus of ₹5.9 lakh crore on average since August. When banks have plenty of cash, they do not need to pay more to attract deposits.

## What can you do this week?

A short checklist:

1. **Find your benchmark.** Your sanction letter or loan statement says whether you are on a repo-linked rate, MCLR or a fixed rate.
2. **Find your reset date.** That is when the change will reach you.
3. **Ask for the choice.** If your bank extends the tenure by default, ask whether a higher EMI suits you better. The RBI's rules give you that option.
4. **Compare your spread.** If your spread is much higher than what your bank offers new customers, ask about a lower rate. Banks usually charge a fee to switch, so check it is worth it.
5. **Watch 2 to 4 December.** The RBI has said cuts are off the table for now, so the next move is a hike or a pause.
