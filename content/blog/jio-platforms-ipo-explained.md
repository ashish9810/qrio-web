**The short answer:** Jio Platforms, the telecom and digital arm of Reliance Industries, is raising up to ₹30,213 crore by selling new shares to the public. That makes it the largest IPO in Indian history, and the demand was heavy.

This is a news summary, not investment advice. Below: what is on offer, where the money goes, what the subscription numbers say and what is still not settled.

## What is the Jio Platforms IPO?

An IPO, or initial public offering, is when a private company sells shares to the public for the first time and lists on the stock exchanges. Jio Platforms owns Reliance's telecom network business and its digital services.

Here are the basic terms, as listed by the IPO tracker [Chittorgarh](https://www.chittorgarh.com/ipo/jio-platforms-ipo/) and cross-checked against [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/stock-market-this-week-jio-ipo-rbi-mpc-minutes-q2-results).

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Issue size | Up to ₹30,213 crore |
| Type | Fresh issue of 27 crore shares, no offer for sale |
| Price band | ₹1,065 to ₹1,119 per share |
| Minimum bid | 1 lot of 12 shares, ₹13,428 at the top of the band |
| Open and close | 21 to 23 October 2026 |
| Allotment | 26 October 2026 |
| Expected listing | 28 October 2026 on NSE and BSE |

Chittorgarh marks the dates as tentative, so treat the listing day as expected, not confirmed.

## How big is it compared with other IPOs?

It is large by any measure. A Business Today report from early October, citing Bloomberg, compared it with the last record holder: [Hyundai Motor India's 2024 IPO raised ₹27,870 crore](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/ipo-corner/story/jio-platforms-ipo-ril-led-telco-targets-rs-11-lakh-crore-valuation-october-listing-likely-559808-2026-10-06). Jio's issue is bigger by roughly ₹2,300 crore.

The same report says the shares on offer are about 2.93% of the company after the issue, based on Bloomberg's calculations. So this is a small slice of a very large business. At the top of the price band, Chittorgarh puts Jio's market value at about ₹10.3 lakh crore.

That valuation is lower than what some people expected. Bloomberg reported that earlier estimates went as high as $130 billion to $170 billion, while the target in early October was about ₹11 lakh crore, or $114 billion. Sources differ on the final figure, so use it as a ballpark.

## Where does the money go?

Because every share is new, the company receives the money. Chittorgarh lists the stated purposes:

- ₹27,500 crore to repay, in full or in part, certain borrowings of Reliance Jio Infocomm, a subsidiary.
- The rest for general corporate purposes.

In plain terms, most of the money is meant to reduce debt, not to fund a new product. I could not find a second source for the exact split, so the figures here come from Chittorgarh's reading of the offer documents. The prospectus is the final word.

## Who is buying?

Indian IPOs reserve shares for different kinds of buyers. Per Chittorgarh, the net offer is split 50% for qualified institutional buyers (large funds and similar investors), 15% for non-institutional investors (big individual and corporate bids) and 35% for retail investors.

Demand was strong in all groups, but not equally so. Chittorgarh's snapshot on the evening of the last day showed the issue at 21.36 times subscribed overall:

| Category | Subscription |
| --- | --- |
| Qualified institutions (excluding anchors) | 67.15 times |
| Non-institutional investors | 13.99 times |
| Retail investors | 2.76 times |
| Employees | 2.91 times |
| Existing shareholders | 7.59 times |

"21 times subscribed" means investors asked for 21 times the shares on offer. Chittorgarh also counted about 1.39 crore applications at that time. Other trackers showed slightly different totals, such as 22.19 times, and the figures were still moving, so expect final numbers to differ a little. Reuters reported that bids topped $77 billion, calling it India's most subscribed IPO, according to its [headline](https://money.usnews.com/investing/news/articles/2026-10-23/ambanis-3-2-billion-jio-indian-ipo-lures-investors-as-bids-surge-on-final-day). I could not open the full article, so I am only relying on the headline claim.

Notice the gap. Institutions asked for far more than retail did. Retail was subscribed under three times, which is healthy but modest next to the institutional demand.

## Why does this IPO matter?

Three reasons stand out.

**It is a test of the market.** The Business Today report says several Indian IPOs had struggled on pricing after a stock market slide, and that the Nifty 50 was down about 15% from its September 2024 peak. A big, well-subscribed listing suggests investors still have money for large deals.

**It changes the index map.** At the proposed valuation, Business Today says Jio would be India's third most valuable listed company, behind Reliance Industries and Bharti Airtel. Large listings can pull money in index funds toward a new name.

**It shows what a "public" Jio looks like.** Until now Jio's numbers have mostly been seen through Reliance's results. A listed Jio has to report on its own, with its own quarterly filings. That will give everyone a clearer view of a telecom business that serves a huge share of the country.

## What does "anchor investor" mean here?

Before an IPO opens to the public, large institutions can be offered shares in advance. These are called anchor investors. Per Chittorgarh, Jio's anchor book was 7.25 crore shares and raised ₹8,109.85 crore, which is a little over a quarter of the total issue. Anchors usually agree to hold for a set period, and their presence is often read as a sign of confidence from big funds. It is a signal, not a guarantee of how the shares will trade.

## What is still not settled?

- **The listing price.** The first-day price is set by the market, not by the issue price. Heavy subscription often helps, but it is no guarantee. Some IPOs with very high demand have still fallen after listing.
- **The final subscription numbers.** The figures above are a snapshot. Exchanges publish the final tally after the close.
- **The exact dates.** Trackers label allotment and listing dates as tentative.
- **Valuation.** Reports give different numbers depending on the date and the exchange rate used.
