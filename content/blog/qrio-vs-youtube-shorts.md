**The short answer:** YouTube Shorts is better if you want range, depth and the biggest library of creators on earth. Qrio is better if you want a feed that only covers business, startups and the world, has no ads, and is designed to be finished rather than scrolled forever.

*Disclosure: we make Qrio. We have tried to be fair to YouTube here, and the settings section below is worth reading even if you never install our app.*

We checked both apps' Google Play listings and YouTube's own help pages on 13 November 2026. Ratings and features change, so treat the details as a snapshot.

## Why compare Qrio with YouTube Shorts at all?

Because most people who want useful short videos already watch them on YouTube. It is the default, it is free, and the good creators are there.

The problem is not the quality at the top. It is everything around it. The same swipe that gives you a clear minute on how a company makes money also gives you a prank, a clipped podcast argument and a cooking video, in whatever order the feed thinks will keep you there. If you have read our post on [why we doomscroll](/blog/why-we-doomscroll), you know that variety is the hook, not a side effect.

So the real question is not "which app has better videos". It is "which app makes it easier to watch the videos you actually wanted".

## What YouTube Shorts does better

**Scale and range.** YouTube CEO Neal Mohan said at Cannes Lions in June 2025 that Shorts were ["averaging over 200 billion daily views"](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/neal-mohan-cannes-2025/), a figure [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/06/18/youtube-shorts-200-billion-daily-views-google-veo-3-ai-neal-mohan/) also reported. Whatever niche you care about, somebody is making Shorts about it.

One honest caveat on that number. From 31 March 2025, YouTube started counting a Shorts view the moment a video starts or replays, with no minimum watch time, as [PPC Land reported from YouTube's own announcement](https://ppc.land/youtube-changes-how-shorts-views-are-counted-from-march-31/). So 200 billion views includes a lot of swiping past. Tubefilter makes the same point.

**Depth when you want it.** A Short can now run up to three minutes, according to [YouTube's help page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en). More usefully, many Shorts creators also make long videos. When a one-minute clip makes you curious, the 20-minute version is one tap away.

**Everywhere you are.** YouTube works on Android, iPhone, laptops and TVs. Qrio is Android only.

**It is getting better at letting you stop.** More on that below.

## What Qrio does better

**A narrow feed by design.** Qrio's [Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.qrio.qrio) describes one-minute videos on business, startups, tech, marketing, geopolitics, science and history, from creators the team picks by hand. There is no entertainment section to drift into. We wrote about [how we pick what goes into a Qrio video](/blog/how-we-pick-topics) if you want the detail.

**No ads.** The listing says Qrio is free and has no ads. YouTube's listing says it contains ads and in-app purchases. That is a fair trade for a free service of YouTube's size, but it does shape what the feed is built to do.

**A small daily goal.** Qrio's "Daily 3" asks you to watch three reels a day and builds a streak around that. It is a different idea from a time limit: a target you can finish, rather than a ceiling you push against.

**What it is not good at.** Being tiny cuts both ways. Qrio has a fraction of YouTube's creators, so you will run out of new videos on a given topic far sooner. It has 20 Play reviews, which is too few to judge it by. If you want a proven product today, YouTube is the safer bet.

## Can you make YouTube Shorts more useful?

Yes, and this is worth doing whatever you decide about Qrio. YouTube has added real controls over the past year.

1. **Set a Shorts feed limit.** In the mobile app go to You, then Settings, then Time management, then Shorts feed limit, as [YouTube's help page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16671528?hl=en) describes. The feature started rolling out on 22 October 2025, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/22/youtube-adds-at-timer-for-you-to-stop-scrolling-shorts) and [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/10/22/youtubes-new-daily-timer-stops-you-from-wasting-all-your-time-watching-shorts/).
2. **Know that it is a nudge, not a lock.** When you hit the limit, a reminder appears that you can dismiss, per the same help page. [ETV Bharat reported in April 2026](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/technology/youtube-adds-shorts-feed-limit-feature-to-help-users-manage-screen-time-enn26041701277) that for teen accounts supervised through Family Link, the limit cannot be dismissed.
3. **Try zero.** The help page lists zero as an option. ETV Bharat reports that setting it removes videos from the Shorts tab entirely, which is the closest thing to switching Shorts off while keeping the rest of YouTube.
4. **Train the feed hard.** YouTube's [recommendations help page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6342839?hl=en) says you can mark any Short "Not interested", choose "Don't recommend channel", or pick "Show fewer Shorts" on the Home grid. Doing this for a week changes the feed more than you would expect.
5. **Pause watch history when you are browsing aimlessly.** The same page says new activity stops shaping your recommendations while history is paused. It is useful if one late night of clips would otherwise take over your feed.

If you want more ideas like these, our guide on [how to stop doomscrolling without deleting your apps](/blog/how-to-stop-doomscrolling) has a longer list.

## Does short video hurt your attention?

It is the question underneath this whole comparison, so here is what the best evidence says.

A meta-analysis led by Lan Nguyen of Griffith University, published in *Psychological Bulletin* in 2025, pooled 71 studies with 98,299 participants. As [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/large-meta-analysis-links-tiktok-and-instagram-reels-to-poorer-cognitive-and-mental-health/) and [Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/s-is-all-that-scrolling-really-dulling-your-attention-researchers-combed-71-studies-the-link-is-real-but-how-compulsively-you-scroll-matters-more-than-how-long/) both report, heavier short video use was most consistently linked to weaker attention and weaker inhibitory control, which is the ability to stop yourself acting on an impulse.

The important caveat: most of those studies were cross-sectional. They show a link, not cause and effect. People who already struggle with attention or stress may simply scroll more. Silicon Canals also notes that how compulsively people scroll seems to matter more than total time.

That is why we do not think the answer is "short video is bad". A minute that explains why a trade deal matters is not the same as forty minutes of random clips, even if both are vertical. Our longer look at [whether short videos are bad for you](/blog/are-short-videos-bad-for-you) goes through more of the research. If scrolling is affecting your sleep, mood or work, talking to a doctor or counsellor is a better step than any app.

## Which one should you use?

Use this as a rough guide:

- **You want everything, including long videos, and you are good at stopping:** stay on YouTube, and set a Shorts feed limit anyway.
- **You like YouTube but the Shorts tab keeps winning:** set the limit to zero and use subscriptions and search instead.
- **You want a short daily habit that only covers business, startups and the world:** try Qrio alongside YouTube, not instead of it.
- **You use an iPhone:** YouTube, for now, because Qrio is Android only.

Many people will end up using both. YouTube for depth and searching a topic, a narrower app for the five minutes on the metro when you would otherwise open Instagram. Our list of [apps to replace your Instagram scroll](/blog/apps-to-replace-instagram-reels) and our comparison of [short video apps for business news in India](/blog/short-video-apps-for-business-news-india) cover the other options fairly.

If you want to see what a Qrio reel looks like before installing anything, the watch page for [when Samsung got people to trick YouTube's algorithm](/watch/when-samsung-got-people-to-trick-youtubes-algorithm) is a fitting one to start with.

The test for either app is the same. A week from now, can you tell a friend one thing you learned from it? If yes, it earned its place on your phone.
