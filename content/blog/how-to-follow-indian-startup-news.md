**The short answer:** the easiest way to keep up with Indian startup news is to use one daily tracker for funding and company news, one deeper publication for the why, and the official filings when a story really matters. That takes about five minutes a day.

You do not need ten newsletters or a feed full of founder posts. You need a small setup that ends, plus a little skill at reading funding numbers. Here is both.

## Why is Indian startup news hard to keep up with?

Mostly because there is so much of it. India's startup base keeps growing, and every new company is a possible headline.

The commerce and industry ministry said more than 55,200 startups were recognised by DPIIT in 2025-26, the highest in any year since Startup India began in 2016, according to reports from [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/centre-recognises-record-55200-startups-in-fy26/) and [Manorama Yearbook](https://www.manoramayearbook.in/current-affairs/india/2026/04/18/govt-recognises-over-55200-entities-as-startups-in-2025-26.html). Both say the total crossed 2.23 lakh as of 31 March 2026.

Recognition is a government label, not a sign that a company is big or funded. Most of these firms will never make the news. But the number explains why your feeds feel endless. There is always another round, another launch, another layoff.

## Which sources cover Indian startups every day?

For the daily layer, you want a tracker. Its job is to tell you who raised money, who shut down and what changed, in a few minutes.

[Inc42](https://inc42.com/) calls itself India's top startup media and intelligence platform. Its site runs daily news, longer features, a weekly startup funding series, a series on company financials called What the Financials, and research reports. It offers a daily news email and a paid membership called Inc42 Plus. The homepage does not show the price.

[Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/) describes itself as a media platform for entrepreneurs, startups and technology enthusiasts. It covers funding rounds and IPOs, and runs weekly and quarterly funding reports. Its Fintrackr section covers company financial results, which is useful when you want numbers instead of claims. It offers a newsletter, though the page does not say whether it is free.

YourStory is another well-known name, with a long record of founder stories. We could not open its site while researching this post, so we will not describe its features. Check it yourself and see if the style suits you.

The large business dailies and sites, such as the Economic Times, Mint and Moneycontrol, also cover startups closely. Our post on [following Indian business news](/blog/best-ways-to-follow-indian-business-news) covers that broader stack.

## Where do I go for the bigger picture?

Daily trackers tell you what happened. A deeper publication tells you whether it matters.

[The Morning Context](https://themorningcontext.com/) says it publishes detailed stories on technology startups, business and economic current affairs. It has dedicated startup and internet sections, with topics like venture capital, quick commerce and fintech. It offers a free subscription and paid plans, including student plans, without listing prices on the homepage.

The Ken is the other well-known Indian name for long startup analysis. We could not open its site today, so see our [business news post](/blog/best-ways-to-follow-indian-business-news), where we looked at it in more detail.

One longer read a week is plenty. Pick a company or sector close to your job, and read one deep piece on it on the weekend.

## How should I read startup funding numbers?

Carefully. This is where most confusion comes from.

Take 2025. Data platform Tracxn reported that Indian tech startups raised $10.5 billion, down 17% from $12.7 billion in 2024, and said India ranked third in the world for startup funding, according to [IANS](https://ianslive.in/india-tech-startups-rank-3rd-globally-in-funding-raise-105-billion-in-2025--20251218165716) and [People Matters](https://www.peoplematters.in/amp/news/funding-and-investment/tech-startup-funding-falls-17percent-to-dollar105-billion-in-2025-report-47773). [Inc42's own year-end count](https://inc42.com/buzz/indian-startup-funding-decline-8-yoy-to-11-bn-in-2025/) put the figure at about $11 billion across 936+ deals, an 8% fall from $12 billion in 2024.

Neither is wrong. They count different things. Tracxn's figure covers tech startups. Each tracker has its own rules on which deals count, when a deal is dated and whether undisclosed rounds are included.

A few habits help:

| When you see | Ask |
| --- | --- |
| "Funding fell X%" | Whose data, and compared with what period? |
| A big round headline | Is it new money, or does it include secondary share sales? |
| A valuation | Did the company say it, or is it an estimate from sources? |
| A weekly total | How many deals, and did one large round drive most of it? |

The simplest rule: compare a tracker with itself over time, not with a different tracker. A weekly Entrackr report, for example, said 28 startups raised about $302.85 million in the week of 12 to 17 October 2026, across growth and early-stage deals ([Entrackr reports](https://entrackr.com/report)). That is useful against last week's Entrackr number, not against someone else's.

## Where can I see the original documents?

Coverage is a summary. For big moments, the original is free and often clearer.

- **IPO plans:** SEBI lists [draft offer documents filed for public issues](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebiweb/home/HomeAction.do?doListing=yes&sid=3&ssid=15&smid=10). When a startup files to list, its draft red herring prospectus shows years of audited numbers, risks and who owns what. Inc42 counted 18 startup listings in 2025, so this page has become more relevant to startup watchers.
- **Policy and schemes:** the [Startup India portal](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/) posts notifications on recognition, tax and regulation, along with schemes like the Seed Fund Scheme and Fund of Funds. It also notes that the turnover limit for recognition was raised from ₹100 crore to ₹200 crore.
- **Data:** [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/) says it tracks more than 8.4 million companies and lets you sign up for free, with paid plans for deeper access.

You will not read these daily. Open them when a company you follow files for an IPO, or a rule change affects your work.

## A five-minute routine that works

Here is a setup you can keep without thinking about it.

1. **Every morning, three minutes:** open one daily tracker, like Inc42 or Entrackr. Scan the headlines. Close it.
2. **Save one story:** if something touches your sector, your employer or your money, save it for later.
3. **Once a week, fifteen minutes:** read one deep piece, or the saved story, properly.
4. **When it really matters:** open the SEBI filing or the Startup India notice and read the first page.
5. **Mute the rest:** founder hot takes and funding screenshots on social media add volume, not much signal.

For the general version of this habit, see [how to stay informed in 5 minutes a day](/blog/stay-informed-in-5-minutes-a-day). If startup news makes you anxious about your job, [following the news without the anxiety](/blog/follow-the-news-without-the-anxiety) may help.

## Where do short videos fit?

They work well as a first pass. A short video can explain what a down round is, or why a company is listing, in under a minute. That makes the written coverage easier to follow later.

They are not the full picture. Videos compress, and compression drops detail. Use them to get the gist, then go to the tracker or the filing for anything you plan to act on.

We make Qrio, a free Android app of short videos on business, startups and the world, so we are biased. Treat it as one option for the first pass, alongside the sources above. Our post on [whether short videos are bad for you](/blog/are-short-videos-bad-for-you) looks at the research fairly.

## What this list leaves out

Plenty of good sources are missing here, including podcasts, regional-language outlets, investor newsletters and the business dailies we did not review in detail. The goal is the shape of a good setup, not a complete ranking. If a source helps you understand the startup world in five calm minutes, keep it.
