**The short answer:** the most useful short video apps for students in India are Khan Academy for school maths and science, Seekho and PW Gyaan-E for practical skills in Hindi or Hinglish, and YouTube for everything else, as long as you set a Shorts limit. If you want business, startups and world affairs instead of syllabus, a small focused app like Qrio fits that gap.

We checked every app's Google Play listing in India on 27 November 2026. Ratings and downloads change every week, so treat the numbers as a snapshot, not a ranking.

*Disclosure: we make Qrio, one of the apps on this list. It is by far the smallest one here, and we say so below.*

## Do students actually use their phones to study?

Many do, but social media wins. The [ASER 2024 report](https://theprint.in/india/education/in-rural-india-82-2-teens-aged-14-16-know-how-to-use-smartphone-but-only-57-use-it-for-studies/2467560), a household survey of rural India released in January 2025, found that 82.2 per cent of 14 to 16 year olds knew how to use a smartphone. Of those, 57 per cent had used it for a study activity in the previous week, while 76 per cent had used it for social media in the same week. [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/aser-report-says-over-56-percent-children-14-16-age-group-use-smartphone-for-educational-purpose) reported the same figures, and added that boys were more likely to report social media use (78.8 per cent) than girls (73.4 per cent).

That survey is rural and covers younger teens, so it is not a perfect picture of a college student in Pune or Bengaluru. But the pattern will sound familiar. The phone is already in your hand. The question is what is on it.

## Are short videos actually good for learning?

Partly. A one-minute video is great for a single idea, a quick revision or getting curious about something new. It is weak for anything that needs practice, like solving equations or writing an essay.

There is also a real concern about attention. A 2025 meta-analysis in *Psychological Bulletin*, [reported by PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/large-meta-analysis-links-tiktok-and-instagram-reels-to-poorer-cognitive-and-mental-health/), pooled 70 studies with 98,299 participants. Heavier short video use was linked to poorer attention and weaker self-control. Two caveats matter. Most of the studies were one-time surveys, so they show a link, not cause and effect. And the studies measured short video in general, not study content in particular. We cover the research in more detail in [are short videos bad for you](/blog/are-short-videos-bad-for-you).

So the useful question is not "short videos, yes or no". It is "which feed, for how long, and for what".

## YouTube: the biggest library, if you can steer it

[YouTube](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.youtube) is where most students already are, and for good reason. Whatever topic you are stuck on, someone has made a video on it, often a very good one, and it costs nothing.

The problem is the feed. The same swipe that gives you a clear minute on the French Revolution gives you a prank next. Two settings help. First, subscribe to a handful of strong channels and use the Subscriptions tab instead of the Shorts tab. Second, set a daily Shorts limit: go to You, then Settings, then Time management, then Shorts feed limit, as [YouTube's help page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16671528?hl=en) describes. You can pick zero. On an adult account it is only a reminder you can dismiss. According to [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/technology/youtube-adds-shorts-feed-limit-feature-to-help-users-manage-screen-time-enn26041701277), on teen accounts supervised through Family Link the limit cannot be dismissed. We compare the Shorts experience in more depth in [Qrio vs YouTube Shorts](/blog/qrio-vs-youtube-shorts).

**Good for:** range, and the best teachers on almost any topic.

**Less good for:** stopping. It is built to keep you watching.

## Khan Academy: the calm option for school subjects

[Khan Academy](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=org.khanacademy.android) is not a reels app, and that is its strength. The listing describes a free app for Class 1 to 12 with videos, exercises and tests, mapped to the NCERT and CBSE syllabus, in Hindi, English and other regional languages. It lists more than 10,000 videos and practice exercises in maths and science, plus over 40,000 practice exercises with hints, and lets you download lessons for offline use.

It shows 4.3 stars from about 1.74 lakh reviews and more than 1 crore downloads. The listing shows no ads.

**Good for:** school students who want to fix a weak concept and then practise it straight away.

**Less good for:** college students, or anyone after current affairs and career skills.

## Seekho: short courses for practical skills in Hindi

[Seekho](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.seekho.android) calls itself a short learning video app with more than 10,000 video courses in Hindi, made by 250+ creators it calls Seekho Gurus. Its trending categories include mobile tricks, online earning, share market, government jobs, English speaking, photography and soft skills.

It shows 4.5 stars from about 13.9 lakh reviews and more than 10 crore downloads. It shows no ads, but has in-app purchases, so expect to pay for full access.

**Good for:** college students and young job seekers who want practical, money and career skills explained in Hindi.

**Less good for:** school syllabus, or anyone who wants content in English.

## PW Gyaan-E: bite-sized skills from Physics Wallah

Physics Wallah is best known for exam coaching, and its main [PW app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=xyz.penpencil.physicswala) is built around full courses for NEET, JEE, UPSC and school boards. That is long-form study, not short video.

Its newer app, [PW Gyaan-E](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=xyz.penpencil.gyaane), is the short one. The listing describes 300+ micro-courses in simple Hinglish, with 2 to 5 minute lessons, quizzes, live masterclasses and certificates powered by PW and Microsoft. Topics include AI and tech skills, Microsoft tools, career skills, money management, English speaking and video editing.

It shows 4.5 stars from about 3,440 reviews and more than 1 lakh downloads, with no ads and in-app purchases. It is much newer and smaller than Seekho, so the catalogue is still growing.

**Good for:** students who want structured, short skill lessons with a certificate at the end.

**Less good for:** anyone who wants a free, open feed to browse.

## TED: longer, but worth the occasional break

[TED](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ted.android) is not short video in the reels sense. Its listing offers more than 3,000 talks on tech, science and psychology, subtitles in over 100 languages, and offline downloads. It shows 4.2 stars from about 2.14 lakh reviews and more than 1 crore downloads, with ads and in-app purchases.

**Good for:** swapping one evening scroll for a single talk that actually stays with you.

**Less good for:** a two-minute break between classes.

## Qrio: business and the world in one minute

[Qrio](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.qrio.qrio) is our app, so weigh this section accordingly. It is a free Android app of 1-minute vertical videos on business, startups, tech, marketing, geopolitics, science and history, from creators our team picks by hand. The listing shows no ads and no in-app purchases. A streak rewards watching at least one video a day, and a Mind Hub maps the topics you have covered. A typical example is our short video on [the bat and ball puzzle most Harvard students got wrong](/watch/the-bat-and-ball-puzzle-most-harvard-students-got-wrong).

The numbers are tiny: 4.9 stars from 22 reviews and just over 100 downloads. It does not cover school syllabus or exam prep.

**Good for:** students preparing for interviews, case competitions, MBA entrance group discussions, or who simply want to understand the business news.

**Less good for:** board exams, NEET or JEE. Use Khan Academy or a coaching app for those.

## What about exam prep apps?

If you are preparing for NEET, JEE, UPSC or CAT, a short video app is a side dish, not the meal. Full coaching apps like [Unacademy](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.unacademyapp) and the main PW app are built around live classes, recorded lectures and mock tests. Unacademy's listing, for example, covers UPSC, IIT JEE, NEET, CAT, GATE and CA, with live classes and test series. Both show no ads but sell courses inside the app.

The edtech market has also been rocky, which is worth knowing before you pay for a long plan. Our story on [Unacademy's sale at 94 per cent below its peak value](/explainers/unacademy-sold-for-94-less-than-what-it-was-once-worth-its-ceo-said-so-himself) explains what happened.

## Which one should you pick?

A simple way to decide:

1. **You are in school and stuck on maths or science:** Khan Academy.
2. **You want practical skills in Hindi:** Seekho.
3. **You want short skill courses with a certificate, in Hinglish:** PW Gyaan-E.
4. **You want the widest choice and can control yourself:** YouTube, with a Shorts limit set.
5. **You want one big idea at a time:** TED.
6. **You want business, startups and world affairs without the rest of the feed:** a focused app like Qrio.

Most students will end up with two: one for their syllabus and one for the curiosity scroll. The trick is making the curiosity app the one on your home screen, and moving the endless feed into a folder.
