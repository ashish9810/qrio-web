**The short answer:** Zetwerk, an Indian company that gets factories to make things for other businesses, raised ₹5,012 crore in its IPO and listed on 23 October 2026. The shares opened about 5% above the issue price, then slid below it on the same day, a sign that investors like the growth but are unsure about the losses and thin margins.

This is a news summary written on 28 October 2026, not investment advice. The share price is still moving, so check the NSE or BSE for the latest figure. Below: what Zetwerk actually does, the IPO terms, what happened on listing day and what the numbers in the prospectus say.

## What does Zetwerk do?

Zetwerk calls itself an "asset-light manufacturing platform". In plain terms, a company that needs steel structures, solar parts, transformers or electronics places an order with Zetwerk. Zetwerk then gets it made, either in one of its own plants or by one of thousands of outside suppliers, and is responsible for delivering it.

According to the company's [abridged prospectus](https://www.morganstanley.com/content/dam/msdotcom/en/indiaofferdocuments/pdfs/Zetwerk_Manufacturing_Businesses_Limited_Abridged_Prospectus.pdf), it had 26 of its own factories across India, Spain, Germany and the USA in FY26, plus 6,979 third-party suppliers in 26 countries. Software it calls Zetwerk OS splits work across that network and tracks it.

The business has two halves:

- **Manufacturing.** Energy products (like parts for renewables and power), precision products (like electronics components) and capital goods for big projects. This brought in about 59% of FY26 revenue.
- **Ecosystem, under the Terra91 name.** Zetwerk buys industrial commodities such as metals in bulk and sells them on to customers. This brought in about 41% of FY26 revenue.

Customers named in coverage by [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/zetwerk-makes-nse-debut-at-5-5-premium-after-%E2%82%B95012-cr-ipo/) and [Indian Startup News](https://indianstartupnews.com/news/rs-15913-crore-revenue-rs-1606-crore-loss-zetwerk-makes-a-5-stock-market-debut-12740921) include Siemens Gamesa, Acer India, boAt and NTPC Renewable Energy. The company was founded in 2018 by Amrit Acharya and Srinath Ramakkrushnan and became a unicorn in 2021, according to both outlets.

## How big was the Zetwerk IPO?

Here are the terms, from the prospectus and cross-checked against Indian Startup News and Inc42.

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Total issue | ₹5,012 crore |
| New shares (money to the company) | ₹2,830 crore |
| Shares sold by existing owners | 14.45 crore shares, about ₹2,182 crore |
| Price band | ₹143 to ₹151 per share |
| Bidding | 15 to 19 October 2026 |
| Anchor investors | About ₹2,251 crore, raised on 14 October |
| Value at ₹151 | About ₹25,583 crore |
| Listing | 23 October 2026, NSE and BSE |

Two details matter here. First, a little over 40% of the issue was an offer for sale, meaning that money goes to existing shareholders, not to Zetwerk. Second, the valuation was below the company's last private round. Indian Startup News reports that round in March valued Zetwerk at roughly ₹27,000 crore. So the public market got a slightly lower price than private investors paid months earlier.

## Who sold shares in the IPO?

The two founders each sold about 1.42 crore shares, according to Indian Startup News, and early funds including Peak XV, Lightspeed, Accel and Kae Capital also sold part of their stakes. Inc42 reports that Kae Capital and Kalysta Capital made 38.3 times their money on the shares they sold.

Indian Startup News also reports that Greenoaks Capital, the largest shareholder at nearly 20%, sold nothing. That is one source, so treat it as reported rather than confirmed.

Early investors selling in an IPO is normal. It is how venture funds return money to their own backers. It does mean that part of the ₹5,012 crore never reaches the company.

## Where does Zetwerk's share of the money go?

Per the prospectus, most of the ₹2,830 crore of new money goes to cutting debt:

1. ₹1,300 crore to repay or prepay borrowings of Zetwerk itself.
2. ₹860 crore to repay borrowings of nine subsidiaries, including Terra91 and its electronics and transformer units.
3. The rest for acquisitions not yet identified and general corporate purposes, capped at 35% of the gross proceeds.

Paying down debt lowers interest costs, which should help profits. The acquisition money is a blank cheque by design, so watch what the company buys.

## What happened on listing day?

The start was good. Zetwerk opened at ₹159.25 on the NSE and ₹159 on the BSE on 23 October, about 5% above the ₹151 issue price, according to [Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/ipos/zetwerk-manufacturing-businesses-ipo-listing/), [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/zetwerk-manufacturing-business-shares-make-decent-debut-stock-lists-with-5-gains) and Inc42.

It did not last. Within the first hour the price fell below ₹151, as [Lapaas Voice](https://lapaasvoice.com/zetwerk-manufacturing-lists-at-5-premium-over-ipo-price-as-shares-slip-below-issue-level/) reported, citing Moneycontrol. Inc42 reports the stock touched a low of ₹127.40 on the NSE and closed the day at ₹138.25, about 8% below the issue price. I found only one source for the closing figure, so treat it as Inc42's number.

In the days since, price trackers have shown big swings in both directions. This is a developing story, and a few days of trading say little about where a stock goes over years.

## Why did the shares slip?

Nobody can say for certain why a price moves on a given day. But the subscription numbers and the prospectus offer some clues.

**Retail investors held back.** The issue was subscribed about 3.06 times overall, per Kotak Neo and Outlook Money. Large institutions bid for about 4.7 times their quota. Retail investors bid for only about 0.73 times theirs, so the retail portion was not fully taken up. Compare that with the [Jio Platforms IPO](/blog/jio-platforms-ipo-explained), which closed for bids days later, where retail demand was well over two times.

**The company is still losing money.** Here are the headline numbers from the prospectus:

| Period | Revenue | Loss, continuing businesses | Total loss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FY24 | ₹12,364 crore | ₹853 crore | ₹918 crore |
| FY25 | ₹11,332 crore | ₹215 crore | ₹371 crore |
| FY26 | ₹15,913 crore | ₹964 crore | ₹1,606 crore |
| April to June 2026 | ₹4,118 crore | ₹64 crore | ₹733 crore |

Revenue grew 40% in FY26 and 55% in the June quarter. But FY26 losses jumped. Indian Startup News reports two big one-time items: a ₹796 crore charge linked to employee stock options and share conversions, and a ₹453 crore hit from shutting its civil infrastructure business. Inc42 describes the same two items. Strip those out and the continuing business is close to break-even, which is the bull case. The bear case is that one-time costs keep turning up.

**Margins are thin.** Adjusted EBITDA, a rough measure of operating profit, was ₹421 crore in FY26, a margin of 2.65% per the prospectus. Gross margin was about 12%. The commodity trading side, which grew to 54% of revenue in the June quarter, appears to earn less than manufacturing: the prospectus puts manufacturing contribution margin at 12.4% in FY26, against 8.2% for the company overall.

**The valuation is not cheap for a loss-maker.** [IndMoney](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/stocks/zetwerk-ipo-listing-analysis-buy-hold-sell) put the price at about 1.64 times sales at the issue price. That is modest for a tech company but rich for a low-margin manufacturer, and the market seems unsure which one Zetwerk is.

## What are the main risks the company itself lists?

Every prospectus has a risk section. Zetwerk's top ones, in its own words summarised:

- It has made losses in every period shown, and some subsidiaries also make losses.
- It depends on outside suppliers. Its top 10 suppliers were about 38% of FY26 expenses.
- Its top 10 customers were about 36% of FY26 revenue, and many do not sign firm long-term orders.
- About 83% of FY26 revenue came from India and 14% from the USA, so trouble in either market hurts.
- Its auditors flagged certain matters in their reports for every period shown.

On the other side, the manufacturing order book stood at ₹12,370 crore at the end of FY26, roughly double its FY24 level, per the prospectus. That is work already won and not yet billed.

## What does it mean for you?

If you do not own the stock, Zetwerk is still worth watching for one reason: it is a test of whether Indian investors will pay startup prices for an old-economy business run with software. Factories, steel and transformers are not glamorous, but India's push into renewables, electronics and defence needs a lot of them.

If you own the shares or are thinking about it, the useful signals over the next year are simple:

- Does the loss from continuing operations stay near zero or turn into a profit?
- Does the order book keep growing faster than revenue?
- Does the low-margin commodity business keep growing faster than manufacturing?
- What does the company buy with the acquisition money?
