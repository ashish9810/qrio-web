**The short answer:** if you want apps like Inshorts, the closest text matches are Way2News and Dailyhunt, and the best one-story-a-day option is Finshots. If you want the same quick format but as video, there is no exact copy yet: YouTube is the default, Public (from the Inshorts team) does short videos of local city news, and a few small apps do business and world news only.

We checked every app's Google Play listing in India on 20 November 2026. Ratings and download counts move every week, so read the numbers below as a snapshot, not a ranking.

*Disclosure: we make Qrio, one of the apps on this list. It is the smallest one here by far, and we say so below.*

## Why do people look for an Inshorts alternative?

Usually for one of three reasons. They want news in their own language. They want a bit more depth than 60 words. Or they want to watch rather than read.

[Inshorts](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nis.app) is still very good at what it does. Its listing says it summarises stories from multiple national and international sources in 60 words or less, in English or Hindi, with "only headlines and facts, no opinions". It shows 4.4 stars from about 6.17 lakh reviews, more than 1 crore downloads, and was updated on 18 November 2026. It carries ads and has in-app purchases.

The video question is the interesting one. In the Reuters Institute's [Digital News Report 2026 for India](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/india), around 58 per cent of respondents said they rely on YouTube for news. The same page notes that video-led platforms keep pulling in both traditional outlets and new creators. So people already watch the news. The question is where to watch it without getting lost.

## Text apps closest to Inshorts

### Way2News: the same idea, in your language

[Way2News](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=sun.way2sms.hyd.com) calls itself "India's largest Hyperlocal short news App". You pick your language and location, and flip through short summaries of local and national news. The listing leans hard on Telugu and Tamil news, and adds job alerts, cricket scores, horoscopes and viral videos.

It shows 4.7 stars from about 12.3 lakh reviews and more than 5 crore downloads, with ads and in-app purchases.

**Good for:** people who want Inshorts-style speed in a regional language, with news about their own district.

**Less good for:** national business and world news in English. The extras (memes, WhatsApp status videos) also make the feed busier than Inshorts.

### Dailyhunt: the biggest catalogue

[Dailyhunt](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.eterno) is an aggregator rather than a summary app. Its listing says it brings news from "5000+ trusted Publishers" in English and 13 Indian languages, from Hindi and Tamil to Bhojpuri and Assamese. Its Xpresso section is the part closest to Inshorts: news "at a glance with images & videos", currently in Hindi and English.

It shows 4.7 stars from about 27.5 lakh reviews and more than 10 crore downloads. There are ads, and a paid Premium tier that removes them and adds magazines and e-newspapers.

**Good for:** reading your usual newspaper's stories in your language, all in one place.

**Less good for:** a calm, finite catch up. Thousands of publishers means a very long feed.

### Google News: when you want both sides of a story

[Google News](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.magazines) is not short news, but it fixes Inshorts' biggest gap. Its Full Coverage view, per the listing, shows how one story is reported across many sources, with videos, local reports, FAQs and a timeline. It shows 3.8 stars from about 19.7 lakh reviews and more than 100 crore downloads.

**Good for:** the one story a week you actually want to understand.

**Less good for:** speed. Headlines link out to full articles, so a quick check can turn into twenty minutes.

### Finshots: one story, done properly

[Finshots](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.finception.finshots.android) sends one finance or business story a day in plain English, which its listing says takes under three minutes to read. It says the reading experience is ad free and that there are no in-app purchases. It shows 4.8 stars from about 20.6 thousand reviews and more than 5 lakh downloads.

**Good for:** the depth Inshorts leaves out, on business and the economy.

**Less good for:** breadth. One story a day means you need something else for the headlines.

## Is there an Inshorts for video?

Not quite. Here are the three honest options.

### Public: short video, but local

[Public](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.cardfeed.video_public) is run by the company behind Inshorts. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inshorts) describes it as a hyperlocal news and video-sharing platform launched by Inshorts, and [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/07/15/india-inshorts-raises-60-million-to-scale-public-social-network-app) reported in July 2021 that Inshorts raised $60 million to scale it.

The app's Play listing describes it as "your city's own local app": short videos about power cuts, traffic, local events, crop prices, jobs and festivals in your area. It is available in ten Indian languages (Hindi, Bengali, Gujarati, Marathi, Tamil, Kannada, Malayalam, Odia, Assamese and Telugu), with no English listed. It shows 4.2 stars from about 11.7 lakh reviews, more than 10 crore downloads, and contains ads.

**Good for:** knowing what is happening in your city, in your language.

**Less good for:** anyone hoping for a video Inshorts about markets, startups or geopolitics. That is not what it covers.

### YouTube: the default, with a catch

[YouTube](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.youtube) is where most Indians already watch news, as the Reuters figure above shows. News channels, business publications and independent creators all post short vertical videos there, and it costs nothing.

The catch is the feed. The same swipe that gives you a clear minute on the budget gives you a prank next. We wrote a fuller comparison, including the Shorts time limit setting, in [Qrio vs YouTube Shorts](/blog/qrio-vs-youtube-shorts).

**Good for:** range. Whatever the story, someone has made a video on it.

**Less good for:** stopping. YouTube is built to keep you watching.

### Qrio: small, and only about business and the world

[Qrio](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.qrio.qrio) is our app, so weigh this paragraph accordingly. It is a free Android app of 1-minute videos on business, startups, tech, geopolitics and history, from creators our team picks by hand. The listing shows no ads and no in-app purchases, and a Daily 3 feature builds a streak around watching three videos a day. A typical example is our [short video on why Netflix called sleep its biggest competitor](/watch/why-netflix-said-its-biggest-competitor-is-sleep).

The numbers are tiny: 4.9 stars from 21 reviews and just over 100 downloads. It is not a breaking news app, and it does not cover local news or live sport.

**Good for:** people who liked Inshorts' business and world sections and want the "why" in a short video.

**Less good for:** live headlines. Pair it with a text app.

## Which one should you pick?

A simple way to decide:

1. **You want Inshorts, but in your language:** Way2News or Dailyhunt.
2. **You want Inshorts, but with more depth:** Finshots for business, Google News Full Coverage for everything else.
3. **You want local city news as video:** Public.
4. **You want national and world news as video:** YouTube, with a time limit set.
5. **You want business and the world as short video, without the rest of the feed:** a smaller focused app like Qrio.

Most people end up with two apps, not one. A fast text app for the morning headlines, and one place for the story that deserves more than 60 words. Our guide to [staying informed in 5 minutes a day](/blog/stay-informed-in-5-minutes-a-day) shows how that routine fits into a busy day, and our earlier list of [short video apps for business news in India](/blog/short-video-apps-for-business-news-india) goes deeper on Seekho and Moneycontrol, which we have left out here.

## Does a short news app actually keep you informed?

Partly. A 60-word card tells you what happened. It rarely tells you why. That is fine for most stories, and a problem for the few that matter to your job or your money.

There is also a quieter cost. The Reuters Institute's [2026 India figures](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/india) show 52 per cent of respondents say they avoid the news sometimes or often, and trust in news overall fell four points to 39 per cent. More apps do not fix that. A smaller, better set does. It is the reason so many of us watch the news on our phones in the first place.
